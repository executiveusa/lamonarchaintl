# La Monarca Editorial Operating System

## North Star

Real interview / local story -> verified place -> La Guía -> curated route -> field-tested walking tour -> paid booking -> repeatable neighborhood routes.

## Operating rule

Nothing becomes public because an agent found it, scraped it, drafted it, or inferred it. Public claims require evidence and an editorial decision.

## Story workflow

1. **Scheduled** — a real interview or local story opportunity is identified.
2. **Interview completed** — the conversation happened; private notes/recording/transcript may be attached.
3. **Source ready** — consent and source material are recorded and available to the editor.
4. **Story draft** — editorial draft exists; AI may assist but may not invent facts or quotes.
5. **Fact check** — names, claims, place details, quotes, links, and material assertions are checked.
6. **Editor approved** — consent, source material, and fact checks are complete. This is a human editorial gate.
7. **Published** — a real article record is linked and the final story is approved for public use.
8. **Route candidate** — optional; only a published story tied to a verified, published place may become narrative material for a walking-tour stop.

## Place workflow

Research -> draft place -> verification queue -> evidence recorded -> verified -> separate publication decision -> La Guía.

Verification can include staff visit, owner/manager confirmation, official-channel verification, location/hours check, or another documented method. Verification evidence remains private.

## Tour workflow

Verified published places -> route draft -> ordered stops -> physical walk -> field-test record -> PASS/NEEDS WORK -> publication -> pricing/booking when commercially ready.

A route is not sellable merely because it exists in software. The physical route must be tested after its current stop set is assembled.

## Human gates

A human is required for:

- consent judgment when interview material is used publicly;
- final verification of places;
- final editorial approval;
- accepting a route field test as PASS;
- setting a real price or booking offer;
- production/domain cutover.

## Agent responsibilities

Agents may:

- prepare interview questions;
- organize transcripts and source notes;
- propose drafts;
- flag unsupported claims;
- create verification checklists;
- prepare bilingual drafts for human review;
- suggest route groupings from verified places;
- calculate route hypotheses from coordinates;
- prepare newsletter/social derivatives after editorial approval.

Agents may not fabricate interviews, quotes, verification evidence, businesses, field-test results, prices, bookings, traffic, or customer demand.

## First real proof

The first end-to-end proof is one real local feature:

real interview -> private provenance -> verified place -> checked bilingual story -> editor approval -> public article -> La Guía listing -> subscriber conversion.

The first commercial proof follows after enough verified places exist to assemble and physically test one coherent route.
